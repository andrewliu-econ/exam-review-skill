# Exam Review Playbook

## Answer structures that earn marks

### Definition questions

Use the compact sequence: **object → formal condition → interpretation → example or contrast**. State the mathematical definition when one exists. Do not spend marks on history unless the prompt asks for it.

### Calculation questions

Use: **given information → model or formula → substitution → result → interpretation**. Keep units and signs visible. State the decision rule before comparing a test statistic with a critical value.

### Diagnostic-problem questions

For heteroskedasticity, autocorrelation, multicollinearity, endogeneity, and model-specification problems, answer with this chain:

1. meaning and violated assumption;
2. consequence for the estimator or inference;
3. detection method and its null hypothesis;
4. remedy or robust alternative;
5. one exam-specific caveat.

### Proof or derivation questions

State assumptions first, derive one line at a time, and name the theorem or limiting result used. Mark where an expectation, probability limit, or variance operation is applied.

### Essay questions

Use a thesis, two or three mechanisms, an application to the prompt, and a short limitation. Do not turn the answer into a lecture summary.

## Modes

### Rapid cram

Deliver:

- a one-page topic map;
- 8-12 answer templates chosen by frequency and prerequisite value;
- 20-40 minute timed drills;
- a final error log.

### Full review

Deliver:

- topic dependency order;
- one worked exemplar per question family;
- a compact formula sheet;
- daily study blocks with outputs and review checkpoints;
- two mixed practice sets.

### Answer drafting

For each question, provide the answer card from `SKILL.md`, plus a scoring-point checklist. If the answer is uncertain, mark the uncertain step rather than hiding it in fluent prose.

### Mock exam

Build questions only from authorized source material and the syllabus. Label the paper synthetic. Match the real paper's section structure, mark allocation, and time limit where known. Provide a rubric and allocate at least as much time to review as to generation.

## Priority model

Rank study targets using four inputs:

- **Exam evidence:** frequency, mark weight, and recency.
- **Learner gap:** self-reported weakness and observed errors.
- **Prerequisite leverage:** whether the topic unlocks several later topics.
- **Study cost:** time needed to reach a reliable answer.

A useful default is to prioritize high-leverage gaps that recur across years. Do not assign fake precision to a weighted score unless the inputs are quantitative and comparable.

## Common scoring traps

- Giving a consequence without naming the violated assumption.
- Giving a test name without stating the null hypothesis.
- Reporting a coefficient without units or economic interpretation.
- Using a one-sided critical value for a two-sided test.
- Confusing an estimator's unbiasedness with its efficiency.
- Treating statistical significance as economic importance.
- Mixing up error term and residual.
- Omitting the conditions required for a consistency or asymptotic result.
- Presenting a memory-based answer as if it came from the source paper.
